The Method

Most coaches fix your form.
Jean fixes why it breaks.

Most shooters plateau because they have been taught what to do — not why they stop doing it under pressure. Jean's approach starts with observation. He watches you shoot before he says a word. Within the first drive, he has identified your difficulties and your way of shooting. Rather than changing your technique, he improves it so it remains truly your own.

Then he corrects it — not theoretically, but with repetition, explaining each time the reason behind the miss, until the right movement becomes instinct.

"Jean has fixed in an afternoon what other coaches couldn't solve in a season."

The Experience

A Day
at Montardy

Morning · 8:00

The Briefing

You start with Jean over coffee. He asks about your shooting history, your goals, and what hasn't worked before. No forms. No questionnaires. A conversation.

Mid-Morning · 10:00

On the Grounds

Out onto 1,235 private acres. Jean watches you shoot — quietly, without interrupting — across two or three different stands. He is building a picture. The diagnosis happens here, in the field.

Afternoon · 14:00

The Work

This is where the session changes. Jean introduces one correction — never more than one at a time. You repeat it until it stops being a thought and starts being a movement.

Evening · 19:00

The Review

Back at the château, Jean walks you through what he saw and what to focus on before the next session. Dinner follows — Périgord cuisine, château wine.
